\begin{abstract}
Dog diets,  as with people diets,  remain as the topic of many 
conversations and
experiments with opinions on many components remaining unsettled.
This work discusses dietary changes to a varying group of dogs
over about 8 years motivated by their initial state which appeared
to be less than ideal. Initially, dog compatible people foods 
such as beef and chicken and carrots were combined with a small
subset of vitamins. Over the years, new vitamins or supplements
were added to explore new hypotheses or tends. While many results were
not surprising, several less obvious or controversial results
were obtained. In any case, appeal to fundamentals such as essential
nutirents seemed to be most important with no real "exotics" being 
needed. Attention to amino acids and mainstream vitamins was first
but the stumbling block during most of this time appears to be essential
fatty acids. Ultimately, soybean oil and maybe krilloil were thought to
be important with the former supplying a good mix of essential oils 
that may be neglected. Both nutrients and anti-nutrients may need 
to be considered
with "bad" or spoiled food containing decay products that may have effects
of anti-vitamins that can not be suppressed with more vitamins. 
This problem points to issues with pre-mixed foods and subtle issues with
meal chemistry including pH which may be best kept low to avoid side reactions
and improve uptake. 
Less obvious 
but important results include the use of vitamin K with heartworm positive dogs
and chronic copper supplementation. 
The most recent diet, with the best subjective health of the existing dogs,
will be considered along with prior attempts. 
\end{abstract}
